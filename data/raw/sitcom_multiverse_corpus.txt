SCENE 1: CENTRAL PERK
[Scene: Central Perk coffeehouse. Chandler, Ross, Joey, Monica, Rachel, and Phoebe.]
CHANDLER: Could this coffee BE any hotter? I think my tongue just suffered second-degree burns.
JOEY: Hey, how you doin'? You can't complain about coffee when it's free.
ROSS: It's not free, Joey. Monica paid for it.
MONICA: Exactly! And I expect everyone to use coasters! We are civilized human beings who respect treated wooden surfaces!
PHOEBE: You know, in my past life in Prague, coasters were considered bad karma. We rested our wooden chalices directly on the mud floor.
RACHEL: Guys, look at this magazine. It says that ninety percent of men can't commit to a relationship because of deep-seated childhood anxieties.
ROSS: That is statistically unsubstantiated! Paleontological evidence indicates hominids formed lifelong pair-bonds for communal offspring defense!
CHANDLER: Yes, and nothing says romantic pair-bonding like dragging a woolly mammoth by its tusks into a cave.
JOEY: Did somebody say mammoth sandwich? Because I'm starving. Joey doesn't share food, but I would share mammoth.
RACHEL: Joey, woolly mammoths went extinct twelve thousand years ago.
JOEY: Well, whose fault is that? Probably Ross and his dinosaur friends.
ROSS: We were on a break from hunting! I mean, we were never hunting dinosaurs! Humans and dinosaurs never coexisted!
CHANDLER: Could your defense mechanism BE any more predictable?

SCENE 2: CALTECH APARTMENT 4A
[Scene: Caltech apartment. Sheldon, Leonard, Howard, Raj, and Penny.]
SHELDON: Technically speaking, Ross Geller's assertion regarding hominid pair-bonding ignores evolutionary sociobiology.
LEONARD: Sheldon, Ross is a fictional character in an alternate sitcom dimension.
SHELDON: That is irrelevant. Scientific accuracy must prevail across all multi-verse configurations! Bazinga!
HOWARD: Speaking of multi-verses, my mother called three times asking if I want brisket or kugel for dinner.
RAJ: Howard, you are a thirty-year-old aerospace engineer. You should be cooking your own chicken biryani.
PENNY: Hey guys, what's up? Sheldon, why are there seven whiteboard equations blocking the hallway?
SHELDON: Penny, I am deriving the quantum wave function of a particle confined within a one-dimensional potential well.
PENNY: Right, and I am deciding whether to drink white wine or red wine with pizza.
LEONARD: Red wine goes better with pepperoni, Penny.
SHELDON: Only if you disregard the tannin oxidation process, Leonard. Honestly, your palate is pedestrian. Bazinga!
HOWARD: I once built an automated cocktail dispenser for zero gravity. It exploded in the test vacuum chamber.
RAJ: It didn't just explode, Howard. It soaked the Dean of Engineering in synthetic gin.

SCENE 3: MONK'S DINER
[Scene: Monk's diner booth. Jerry, George, Elaine, and Kramer.]
JERRY: What is the deal with airline peanuts? Have you ever tried to open one of those miniature nitrogen-flushed foil pouches at thirty thousand feet?
GEORGE: Jerry, I can't even open a childproof aspirin bottle without weeping! My hands are delicate! I was born for velvet robes, not manual labor!
ELAINE: George, you worked as an assistant to the traveling secretary for the Yankees. You handed out promotional baseball caps!
GEORGE: That was high-stress logistics, Elaine! High-stress!
KRAMER: (sliding in) Jerry! You've gotta invest in my new business venture!
JERRY: What business venture, Kramer?
KRAMER: The tie-dispenser! You go to a restaurant, you spill marinara on your necktie—bam! A vending machine dispenses a fresh silk four-in-hand tie!
GEORGE: Nobody wears ties to restaurants anymore!
KRAMER: Giddyup! The corporate elite wears ties, George! The movers and the shakers!
ELAINE: These pretzels are making me thirsty!
JERRY: See, that's observational comedy. Simple, direct, undeniably neurotic.
GEORGE: I am neurotic, Jerry! I am the king of neurosis! If neurosis were an Olympic event, I'd take home the silver medal because I'd choke on the gold!

SCENE 4: SITCOM CROSSOVER EXTRAVAGANZA
[Scene: A surreal cafe where the sitcom dimensions collide.]
CHANDLER: Could this multiverse BE any more crowded?
SHELDON: According to the Everett many-worlds interpretation of quantum mechanics, this superposition of comedic protagonists was inevitable.
JOEY: How you doin', Sheldon? You look like you need a slice of pepperoni pizza.
SHELDON: I do not consume unsolicited carbohydrates from strangers. Furthermore, your posture suggests deficient lumbar support.
GEORGE: (to Chandler) You think you have problems? My parents are moving into a retirement community in Florida, and they expect me to drive the U-Haul!
CHANDLER: I would rather assemble fifty Swedish flat-pack entertainment units than drive a U-Haul to Florida.
KRAMER: Florida! The sunshine state! The citrus groves! I knew a guy in Boca Raton who trained pelicans to retrieve lost golf balls!
ROSS: (shouting) Pivot the golf balls! Pivot!
MONICA: Who left this coffee mug without a coaster on the mahogany table?!
SHELDON: It was not I. My coaster placement adheres to strict Cartesian coordinates.
JERRY: And so the universe folds in upon itself, one sarcastic punchline at a time.
